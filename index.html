RUŌOD Lab

Perfume formulation software for people who work in grams.

RUŌOD Lab holds your raw-material library, your formulas and your bench notes on your own device — and does the arithmetic that sits between them. Dilution, composition, scaling and production targets all come from one calculation engine, so the numbers on every screen agree.

What it is

A formulation bench, not a spreadsheet

Most perfumers end up with a materials list in one file, formulas in another, and the percentages recalculated by hand every time something changes. RUŌOD Lab keeps the three connected: a material is a record, a formula is built from those records, and every figure the app shows is derived from them when you look at it.

Everything is derived

How many formulas use a material, how many copies a formula has, what a collection contains — none of it is stored as a number that can go stale. It is worked out from your records each time, so an edit is reflected immediately and a restored backup tells the truth about what it brought back.

One calculation engine

The formula editor, the scaling screen, the status bar, the reports and the exported PDF all read the same engine. No screen recomputes a percentage of its own, which is why a printed total can never disagree with the one on the bench.

Yours, on your device

The application stores your library locally and works with no connection at all. There is no RUŌOD account and no server holding your formulas. Backup to Google Drive exists, and it is something you switch on.

The workflow

From a delivery of materials to a batch you can weigh out

The application is arranged around the order the work actually happens in.

Organise your materials

Record what you have, file it into collections, and keep the supplier, cost, quantity and IFRA limit with it.

Build a formula

Add materials as entries, give each a weight and a dilution, and watch the composition resolve as you type.

Calculate and scale

Read the three percentages, check the IFRA position, and scale the whole formula to a weight, a factor or a concentration.

Document and protect

Write it up in Notes, export it as a PDF or a spreadsheet, and back the whole library up.

Materials

A raw-material library that holds what you actually record

A material in RUŌOD Lab is a full record, not a name and a price. Materials are filed into collections, and the library is searchable, filterable and sortable on the fields you keep.

What a material carries

Name, primary and secondary fragrance family, position in the fragrance pyramid, a picture, CAS number, botanical name, supplier, date acquired, quantity, cost, IFRA limit, dilutions, a solvent flag, your own code and a description. Not every field has to be filled, and fields left empty can be kept out of the way.

Fields you decide on

Material Layout decides which sections appear and in what order; Material Fields decides what a field is and how it behaves. You can add a field that every material carries, or one that belongs to a single material — and either way it travels with the record through a backup.

Finding things

Search each list directly, or search the whole library from one field in Tools. Filter materials by fragrance pyramid position or primary family, and sort by name, date acquired, IFRA limit or quantity. A value that was never recorded sorts last rather than pretending to be zero.

Used in formulas

Each material reports the formulas that use it, counted from the live formula list rather than a stored figure. Distinct formulas, never entries — a formula using a material on three rows counts once, and renaming a material never changes the answer.

Formulas

A formula editor that recalculates as you weigh

A formula is a set of entries, each pointing at a material in your library and carrying a weight and a dilution. Everything else about it is worked out.

Composition, live

Total weight, total pure active, total internal solvent and total cost resolve as you edit a row. The status bar above the entries shows the measures you chose to keep in front of you, from eleven the engine derives.

Organisation that survives

Formula collections, a status, tags, a favourite mark and a lock. A locked formula cannot be deleted — a bulk delete containing one is refused whole rather than partly obeyed, and it names which ones are locked.

Versions and copies

Duplicating a formula names the copy and marks it. The version mark never renumbers — delete v2 of four and v4 stays v4 — while the duplicate count is derived, so it falls when a copy goes.

IFRA limits in view

Where you have recorded an IFRA limit for a material, the engine checks it against that entry's relative percentage and flags the row. A material with no limit recorded is never quietly treated as compliant.

Undo across the app

Edits are undoable and redoable app-wide, not just in one screen, and the depth of the history is yours to set. The history is session data — it is deliberately not carried into a backup.

Print it properly

A formula exports as a laid-out PDF: the ingredient table with its dilutions, the totals beneath it, and the same status-bar measures you selected — typeset rather than dumped.

Dilution & calculation

Three percentages, three different questions

They are routinely treated as one number, and they are not interchangeable. RUŌOD Lab keeps them apart because they answer different things — and because only one of them is the right basis for an IFRA check.

How each percentage is derived for one entry.
Percentage Derived from The question it answers
Formula % entry weight ÷ total weight What physical share of the mass is this row? This is what you read when you are weighing.
Absolute % pure active ÷ total weight How concentrated is this material in the finished mass, once its own solvent is discounted?
Relative % pure active ÷ total pure active What is this material's olfactory contribution? Solvent is ignored entirely, and this is what IFRA limits are checked against.

Pure active and internal solvent

A material entered at a dilution brings solvent into the formula with it. The engine separates the two for every row — pure active is the weight times the dilution, and internal solvent is what is left of the row. Totalled across the formula, they are what makes a concentration figure mean anything.

Internal solvent is not free solvent

Solvent carried inside a diluted material and solvent added to the formula as its own entry are different things, and the application keeps them distinct. An entry you mark as a solvent carries no active weight by definition, which is also what lets scaling draw it down when a concentration has to change.

Changing a dilution: three methods, three things held constant

Switching a row to a different dilution is not one operation. Which of the three you want depends on what must not move.

Method Held constant What moves
Preserve weight The row's weight Pure active shrinks, internal solvent grows. The formula total is unchanged and no other entry is touched.
Preserve percentage The row's pure active Only this row's weight changes, so the formula total moves. The material keeps its relative contribution.
Exchange solvent Pure active and the formula total The row grows and the entries you flagged as solvents are drawn down by the difference.

Direct editing and scaling are different operations. Editing changes a formula's composition — you are deciding that a row should now be a different weight. Scaling transforms the whole formula while holding its proportions, so it answers a different question: how do I make more of the same thing, or the same thing at a different strength?

Scaling & production targets

Take a trial to a batch

The Dilutions & Scaling screen works on the whole formula. Ingredient proportions, dilution percentages and dilution methods are preserved through a scale; what changes is the size or the strength.

Scale to a size

Scale the formula until its total raw weight reaches a target, or apply a factor directly. You can also scale from one ingredient — set the weight or the percentage a single material should have, and the rest of the formula follows it.

Scale to a concentration

Set the absolute percentage the formula should reach, and free solvent is adjusted to meet it while existing dilutions and internal solvent stay intact. A target stronger than the formula could reach with no free solvent at all is refused rather than approximated.

Production targets

Give the finished batch size and the concentration you want it at. The engine returns the concentrate required, the alcohol to make it up with, and the factor between that and the formula as it currently stands.

Calculated before applied

A scaling operation is calculated and shown to you first, and applied only when you accept it. If it does turn out wrong, the app-wide undo is behind you.

Formula Hub

Arithmetic over your own material fields

Separate from the formula engine above, and for a different job: defining your own calculations over the fields your materials carry. Write them once in the Formula Builder and they appear as values on the material.

Material

Arithmetic over one material's own fields — [Cost] × 3. Saved results become calculated fields in the material field catalogue. An empty source makes the result unavailable rather than zero; nothing is written to a material to make a calculation succeed.

Aggregates

SUM, AVERAGE, MIN, MAX and COUNT across every material in the library. Here COUNT reports how many materials actually hold a value, so you can see what the average was taken over.

Derived

Calculations that build on saved aggregates and on each other by name. Dependencies are tracked: a calculation something else depends on cannot be deleted, and a circular reference is refused when you save it.

Notes

Where the reasoning goes

My Notes

Your own notes, written with headings, bullet lists, bold, italic and underline. File them into collections, give them a category, pin the ones you keep returning to, and reorder them by hand. Notes export as a PDF, individually or in a selection.

App Notes

Everything you have already written into a description — on a material, a formula or either kind of collection — gathered into one readable list. It is a view over those records rather than a second copy, so editing the record is editing the note.

Notes are yours alone and stay on your device. RUŌOD Lab has no shared documents, no comments and no real-time editing — it is a bench notebook, not a collaboration tool.

Tools & reports

Questions about the library, not just one record

Every statistic the application showed before Reports was about a single material or a single formula. The Reports Center answers eight questions about the whole library — computed when you open it, stored nowhere.

The eight reports

Library composition. What is recorded and what is missing. Which materials are most used, and which are orphans. Every IFRA breach across every formula. Stock value and unit costs. Where materials come from. The status and complexity of the formula portfolio. And what the library smells of, by family and pyramid position.

Nothing is recorded as zero

A material priced at zero is valued; one with no price recorded is excluded and counted separately. Out of stock and quantity-not-recorded are reported as different rows. A material with no IFRA limit recorded is named as unknown, never counted as compliant. Every figure carries its denominator.

Suppliers & families

Keep your supplier list and your fragrance families in one place, and use them from the material editor, the filters and the reports alike.

Global search

One field searches materials, formulas, both kinds of collection and both kinds of note. What each search reads — names, descriptions, field values, tags — is yours to configure, separately for global and in-list search.

The RUŌOD Library

A built-in library of materials, formulas and nineteen primary families you can generate to start from, and clear when you no longer want it. It stays distinguishable from your own records for as long as you keep it.

Your data

Import it, export it, back it up

A formulation library represents years of work. RUŌOD Lab is built so you can always get it out again.

Export

Materials, formulas and collection indexes export as PDF, CSV, Excel or JSON. Export one record, the selection you have made, or the whole library — the design of the document does not change with the number of records in it.

Import

Bring an existing library in from CSV, Excel or JSON. If you are moving between devices, a complete backup file is the better route — and the importer says so rather than partly reading one.

Backup

One backup package, two destinations: your device's own storage, or your Google Drive. Both write the same file, and it carries your records whole — including the pictures you took, as bytes rather than as paths that would mean nothing on another install.

What backup is, and what it is not. RUŌOD Lab does not synchronise. Your library lives on your device; a backup is a snapshot you choose to write, and Google Drive is one of the two places you can put it. Connecting Drive is optional, the application only ever reaches the files it created there, and restoring replaces your current data rather than merging with it — which is exactly why it asks first, and says what it is about to replace. Details are in the Privacy Policy.

Questions

Frequently asked

What is RUŌOD Lab?

A perfume formulation and laboratory-management application for Android. It holds your raw-material library, your formulas and your notes on the device, and calculates dilution, composition, scaling and production targets from one engine.

Does RUŌOD Lab work offline?

Yes — it is offline-first. Your materials, formulas, notes and settings are stored on your own device, and every screen works with no connection. A connection is only needed if you choose to back up to Google Drive.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no RUŌOD account and no sign-up. The only sign-in anywhere in the application is Google's own, and only if you decide to connect Google Drive for backup.

How does dilution work?

Each row carries a raw weight and a dilution percentage. From those the engine derives the pure active weight and the internal solvent the row brought with it, and totals both across the formula. Changing a row's dilution is done through one of three methods, which differ in what they hold constant — the row's weight, the row's pure active, or both the pure active and the formula total.

Can I scale a formula for production?

Yes. Scale to a target total weight, by a factor, from a single material's weight or percentage, or to a target absolute percentage. For a finished batch, give the amount and the concentration and the engine returns the concentrate needed, the alcohol to make it up with, and the scaling factor from the formula as it stands.

Does it check IFRA limits?

Where you have recorded a limit for a material, the engine compares it against that entry's relative percentage and flags the row, and one of the eight reports gathers every breach across every formula. It checks what you have recorded — RUŌOD Lab does not ship IFRA standards and is not a substitute for the current published standards or for your own regulatory assessment.

Where is my data stored, and can I back it up?

On your device. Settings has one Backup section with two destinations — your device's own storage and your Google Drive — and both write the same complete package. Google Drive backup is optional and starts only once you connect an account. You can also export your library as PDF, CSV, Excel or JSON at any time. The Privacy Policy sets out exactly what is stored where.

Is RUŌOD Lab free?

The application includes a subscription, RUŌOD Lab Premium, sold through Google Play Billing. No feature is currently behind it — every screen behaves the same whether or not you subscribe. Google Play is the authority on price in your country.

Is there an iPhone or desktop version?

Not at present. RUŌOD Lab is an Android application, and this website is its product site rather than a version of the app you can use in a browser.

Can I work on a formula with someone else?

No. RUŌOD Lab is a single-user application with no shared workspace, no comments and no real-time editing. Sharing is done by exporting a PDF or a spreadsheet.
